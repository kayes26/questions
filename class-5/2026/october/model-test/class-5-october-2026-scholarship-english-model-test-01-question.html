Textbook-based passage — Unit 7: The Sundarbans (adapted)

The Sundarbans is a mangrove forest in the southwestern part of Bangladesh. Its strong tree roots hold the soil and reduce coastal erosion. The forest stretches across areas of Khulna, Satkhira and Bagerhat. UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site in 1997. Many animals live there, including the Royal Bengal Tiger, spotted deer, crocodiles and dolphins. Families living nearby collect honey and other forest products to earn a living. The forest also protects coastal communities from cyclones and tidal waves. We should conserve this valuable habitat for future generations.

Unseen passage for Questions 4–5

When a flood damaged the only footbridge to Charpara village, eleven-year-old Nila noticed that younger children were missing school. The nearest safe crossing was four kilometres away. Instead of complaining, she interviewed the villagers and wrote down their concerns. Her teacher helped her prepare a letter to the local council. Within a week, the council sent an engineer to inspect the broken bridge. Repairs could not begin immediately because the river was still rising. Meanwhile, volunteers arranged a supervised boat service for schoolchildren. Nila kept a daily record of how many children used it. A month later, the repaired bridge reopened. At a community meeting, Nila thanked the volunteers and reminded everyone that small actions, when planned carefully, can solve serious problems.
